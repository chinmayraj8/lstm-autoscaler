## What's confirmed so far (updated after Step 18)
- **Empirical per-machine gating now exists and reproduces this project's own manual audit judgment automatically (Step 18).** `src/autoscaler/shadow.py`'s `decide_assignment`, replayed against Step 16's real per-seed cost data, recommends the hybrid only for m_2104 and correctly withholds it from m_2380/m_2647 (and every "no advantage" machine checked) — even though m_2380/m_2647's 5-window aggregate verdict alone reads "confirmed cheaper," matching what a naive mean-only check would wrongly conclude. The harness's strict requirement that the hybrid win *every* banked window (not just on average) is what catches the distinction Step 16's audit needed a human to find by hand (the individual seed on which each of those two machines ties ARIMA exactly). This is a shadow-mode measurement-and-gating harness with repeat-window statistical gating — explicitly not a bandit, not continuously adaptive, and not yet wired to any live ARIMA/hybrid forecasting pipeline (that doesn't exist as a service component; only as the offline Step 16 script). 60/60 project tests pass (35 pre-existing + 25 new, synthetic-data-only).
- **m_2104's mechanism is unknown — the natural hypothesis (leftover ARIMA residual autocorrelation) is not just unconfirmed, it's inverted (Step 17).** m_2104 has the whitest ARIMA(2,0,1) residuals of all 13 machines (Ljung-Box p=0.99 at lag 10, lowest max|ACF| in the first 5 lags) — the exact opposite of "ARIMA underfits here, leaving structure for the LSTM." A follow-up nonlinear/ARCH-effect check (Ljung-Box on squared residuals) doesn't rescue the story either: volatility clustering is nearly universal across all 13 machines regardless of hybrid outcome, and m_2104's own squared-residual significance is *less* extreme than both "thin" machines' (backwards from what the hypothesis would predict). None of burstiness, `demand_scale`, K-means cluster membership, or val→test demand-ceiling growth cleanly separate the 3 winning machines from the other 10 either. No combination-of-features search was attempted (only 3 positive examples in a 13-machine sample — would be overfitting, not a finding). **Honest conclusion: no predictor found. "m_2104 is real but we don't yet know why" is the place this investigation lands** — the underlying Step 16 result doesn't need a mechanism to be trusted (it stands on 5/5 seeds, zero generalization gap, and robustness to cluster-mate parameter swaps), but nothing here tells you *in advance* which future machine would show the same effect. A draft (unimplemented) production recommendation follows from this: default to plain ARIMA for all machines (simpler, deterministic, already wins/ties on 9/13), treat the hybrid as an opt-in enhancement gated by empirical per-machine validation rather than a static rule, since no such rule exists.
- **m_2104 is the first LSTM-related result in this project's 16-step history to survive the full confound-audit treatment (Step 16, residual hybrid).** Combining ARIMA and the LSTM — training the LSTM to predict ARIMA(2,0,1)'s residuals from the same lookback window, rather than pitting the two head-to-head — produces 4/13 "confirmed cheaper than ARIMA" machines. One (m_2085) is trivially explained: it beats an already-known-anomalous ARIMA number (Step 10's confound), not a hybrid-specific finding. Of the 3 new candidates, **m_2104 survives every check this project has developed since Step 10**: val→test demand growth runs the safe direction, its val-tuned params are exactly its own test-optimal (zero generalization gap), all 5 individual seeds beat ARIMA (not just the mean), and the win survives being handed all 4 cluster-mates' independently-tuned params instead of its own. m_2380 and m_2647 show a real but thin, partially fragile signal (real generalization gaps of 0.024-0.031; some cluster-mate substitutions tie or lose). A simpler linear blend of the two standalone forecasts (`w`-tuned) mostly just rediscovers "trust ARIMA" (w near 0 on 9/13 machines, only 1/13 confirmed win) — informative in its own right: raw-forecast averaging exposes little complementary signal, so whatever the LSTM adds is concentrated specifically in modeling ARIMA's residual structure. **This narrows, but does not overturn, Step 15's broader "LSTM isn't the right tool at this scale" conclusion**: on the raw series, extra features, or pooled data, the LSTM adds nothing that survives scrutiny; given the narrower, more tractable problem of ARIMA's residuals specifically, it can extract a small amount of genuine signal on at least one machine, probably a couple more.
