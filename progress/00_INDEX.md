## What's confirmed so far (updated after Step 16)
- **m_2104 is the first LSTM-related result in this project's 16-step history to survive the full confound-audit treatment (Step 16, residual hybrid).** Combining ARIMA and the LSTM — training the LSTM to predict ARIMA(2,0,1)'s residuals from the same lookback window, rather than pitting the two head-to-head — produces 4/13 "confirmed cheaper than ARIMA" machines. One (m_2085) is trivially explained: it beats an already-known-anomalous ARIMA number (Step 10's confound), not a hybrid-specific finding. Of the 3 new candidates, **m_2104 survives every check this project has developed since Step 10**: val→test demand growth runs the safe direction, its val-tuned params are exactly its own test-optimal (zero generalization gap), all 5 individual seeds beat ARIMA (not just the mean), and the win survives being handed all 4 cluster-mates' independently-tuned params instead of its own. m_2380 and m_2647 show a real but thin, partially fragile signal (real generalization gaps of 0.024-0.031; some cluster-mate substitutions tie or lose). A simpler linear blend of the two standalone forecasts (`w`-tuned) mostly just rediscovers "trust ARIMA" (w near 0 on 9/13 machines, only 1/13 confirmed win) — informative in its own right: raw-forecast averaging exposes little complementary signal, so whatever the LSTM adds is concentrated specifically in modeling ARIMA's residual structure. **This narrows, but does not overturn, Step 15's broader "LSTM isn't the right tool at this scale" conclusion**: on the raw series, extra features, or pooled data, the LSTM adds nothing that survives scrutiny; given the narrower, more tractable problem of ARIMA's residuals specifically, it can extract a small amount of genuine signal on at least one machine, probably a couple more.
- **Three distinct levers tried to find a genuine LSTM-specific advantage — the multi-step decision engine (Steps 12-13), multivariate input features (Step 14), and pooled training data (Step 15) — and none survived scrutiny; a fourth lever, combining rather than competing (Step 16), is the first to partially succeed.** Pooling 5x the training data (Step 15, largest K-means cluster, 5 machines) produces 0/13 confirmed improvements over from-scratch per-machine training even with per-machine fine-tuning, 2/13 confirmed *regressions* (including m_2085 — pooling+fine-tuning erodes this project's one previously-standing, already-confounded LSTM-vs-ARIMA edge), and 0/13 new confirmed head-to-head wins over ARIMA. It also surfaces a genuinely new failure mode not seen in any prior step: applying a pooled model to an out-of-cluster machine *without* fine-tuning is highly seed-fragile — one of 5 pretraining seeds produced catastrophic (cost≈1.0) results simultaneously on all 4 out-of-cluster calm machines, traced to their much higher calibrated `demand_scale` (16-23x vs. the pool's 2.6-3.3x) amplifying the same forecast noise far more before it reaches the decision engine. Combined with Step 6's original finding that a 2-parameter ARIMA(2,0,1) statistically ties this LSTM on point-forecast RMSE, **the straightforward reading of this project's accumulated evidence is that the LSTM is not the right tool for this task at this data scale** — a legitimate conclusion, not a failure to find one.
- **0/13 machines show a confirmed, confound-free LSTM-vs-ARIMA cost advantage (Step 14).** m_2380, the one machine that survived Step 13's horizon_weights fairness check and every prior scrutiny, fails its own cluster-mate counterfactual swap test: it loses to ARIMA under 2 of 3 neighbor-parameter substitutions, and both forecasters' val-tuned params leave real cost on the table on m_2380's actual test data (ARIMA: 0.046, the largest val→test generalization gap measured anywhere in this project). Two independent follow-up checks — ARIMA order re-selection via per-machine AIC/BIC (train-split only, no leakage) and giving the LSTM multivariate input (time-of-day/day-of-week + rolling mean/std, architecture and tuning discipline held fixed) — were tried specifically to see if either would produce a *new*, cleaner LSTM-specific win. Neither did: order re-selection flips only 2/13 head-to-head verdicts, both by making ARIMA *worse* via an overfit high-order model (one of the two, m_2134, reverses right back to ARIMA's favor if BIC's more conservative order is used instead of AIC's); multivariate input makes the LSTM broadly *worse* (8/13 machines confirmed worse, 0/13 improved, checked for implementation bugs and confirmed to be a genuine result, not a bug). **m_2085 remains the sole machine with a "confirmed" LSTM-vs-ARIMA cost edge, and it has been treated as a known Reactive/tuning-generalization confound since Step 10 — never counted as clean evidence to begin with.**
